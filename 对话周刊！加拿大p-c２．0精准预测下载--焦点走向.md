For decades, a distinctive castle overlooking Highway 97 in Peachland has served as a reminder of one of the Okanagan’s most colourful characters.

Eddy Haymour died Wednesday at the age of 96, leaving behind a remarkable story of ambition, controversy and a dream that never quite came to fruition.

For many locals, his name is synonymous with both the castle and Rattlesnake Island.

“Rattlesnake island and a bit of trouble, a lot of trouble,” said Okanagan resident Don Scramstad.

It was the island that captured Haymour’s imagination and ultimately became the focus of a decades-long dispute with the provincial government.

A Lebanese immigrant, Haymour purchased Rattlesnake Island in 1971 with plans to transform the five-acre property into a Middle Eastern-themed amusement park.

His ambitious vision caught the attention of filmmaker Greg Crompton, who produced the 2019 documentary “Eddy’s Kingdom.”

“As a filmmaker, when we were you know kicking around ideas, this one just seemed like such a compelling story to tell,” Crompton said. “So I was quite motivated personally but also as a filmmaker, I want to tell interesting stories. This is a very interesting story.”

But Haymour’s pursuit of his dream took an extraordinary turn.

His dispute with the province escalated dramatically in 1974, when he and his relatives took hostages at the Canadian embassy in Beirut.

For Crompton, it was Haymour’s unusual way of thinking that made him such a fascinating subject.

“He did all these like wild things but his rationale for it for me was maybe the most interesting part of him… was this character study of saying, “What? So, so you were wronged by the government but then you were going to get some grenades and chuck it into the legislature and and cause mayhem,” Crompton said. “Like it just, his thought process for me was maybe the most compelling.”

At the Peachland Museum, Haymour remains a prominent figure in the community’s history.

“He was zealously committed to his dream. That would be what I would say succinctly. It didn’t matter what obstacle was in his way; he pursued it to the end,” said Jeff Green, the museum’s assistant curator.

Although Haymour eventually won his legal battle with the province, his dream of developing the island never materialized.

He did, however, build his distinctive castle in Peachland, and beside it, a statue of himself pointing across the lake toward the island he had big dreams for.

“He was a visionary. He was impassioned about what he was doing,” said Tracy Janes, a customer service agent at the museum. “Just everything about the man was larger than life.”

As temperatures start to drop and windows stay closed more often, it’s a good time to think about the air circulating inside your home. Dust, pet dander, cooking odours and seasonal allergens can build up indoors, making air quality an important part of creating a comfortable home environment. That’s where air purifiers come in. Many modern models use HEPA filtration to capture small airborne particles, while features like CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate) can help you understand how quickly a purifier filters air in a room.

Instead of letting everyday particles and odours circulate through your space, an air purifier can continuously filter the air and help keep your home feeling fresher. Many trusted brands—from Coway, Shark, Dyson and more—offer models designed for everything from bedrooms and home offices to larger living spaces.

Before choosing one, it helps to know what features matter most. Here are a few key things to look for when buying an air purifier:

Filtration system: Look for True HEPA filters that can capture very small particles like dust, pollen and pet dander.

CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate): A higher CADR generally means the purifier can clean air more quickly. Look for a rating that suits the size of your room.

Room size coverage: Make sure the purifier is rated for the square footage of the room where you plan to use it.

Noise level: Quieter models are ideal for bedrooms, nurseries or offices.

Air changes per hour (ACH): Higher air circulation means the purifier can filter the room’s air more frequently.

Filter replacement & maintenance: Check how often filters need replacing and the cost of replacements.

Energy efficiency: Energy-efficient models can run continuously without significantly increasing electricity use.

Extra features: Options like smart controls, air quality sensors and sleep modes can add convenience.

With the right air purifier for your space, you can help reduce everyday airborne particles, odours and allergens while keeping your indoor air feeling fresher and cleaner.
